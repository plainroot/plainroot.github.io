Privacy Policy — Uzak

Last updated September 24, 2026.

Uzak does not collect anything. There is no account, no server it talks to, and no analytics built into it. The only network traffic it ever makes is to Apple, to handle a purchase — covered below under Purchases. Everything else happens entirely on your phone.

What stays on your phone

Your focus sessions, the city you've grown, and whether you've bought the paid version are stored only in the app's own storage on your device. That storage is shared with the parts of the app that keep working when it isn't open — the shield shown over a blocked app, the button that lifts it, and the piece that ends a session on schedule — so they can act without you reopening the app. That sharing is Apple's ordinary way for an app's own components to see the same data, not a network connection, and none of it is visible to us.

While a session runs, a Live Activity shows its progress on your lock screen. It is drawn on your phone from the session already stored there; it is not sent to us or to Apple, and the app does not ask for a push token to update it.

Blocking apps

To lock your screen during a session, the app uses Apple's Screen Time system (Family Controls). It asks Apple's system to shield your apps as a whole — it does not ask which apps you actually have, and it never receives a list of them. You approve this once, in a system dialog, and can take it back at any time from this app's own Settings screen — the "Turn off Screen Time" switch there ends any running session, unshields every app, and hands the permission back.

Purchases

If you buy the paid version, Apple's own purchase system handles the transaction. We never see your card, your name, or your Apple ID — the app only ever learns a single yes or no: whether you own it. That flag is stored the same way, and in the same place, as everything above.

Backups

The app does not mark its data to be skipped by device backups. So if you back up your phone to iCloud or a computer, that backup most likely includes this small amount of local data, the same as it would for most other apps' local storage. We could not confirm Apple's exact current policy on this from their own documentation while writing this page, so treat this as our honest best understanding rather than a guarantee.

Links that leave the app

A few places in the app hand a link to your browser or mail app: this policy, Apple's standard terms, and the address below. Opening one is a request made by that other app on your behalf, not by us, and we see nothing of it.
