Privacy Policy — Uzak

Last updated September 26, 2026.

Uzak has no account and no server of its own. It never sees which apps you use, and nothing from Apple's Screen Time system — no app names, no usage figures, nothing about the apps you block — is ever sent anywhere. A few outside services do receive a small amount of data from the app. Every one of them is listed below, with what it gets, why, and whether you can switch it off.

What stays on your phone

Your focus sessions and the city you've grown are stored only in the app's own storage on your device. That storage is shared with the parts of the app that keep working when it isn't open — the shield shown over a blocked app, the button that lifts it, and the piece that ends a session on schedule — so they can act without you reopening the app. That sharing is Apple's ordinary way for an app's own components to see the same data, not a network connection, and none of it is visible to us.

While a session runs, a Live Activity shows its progress on your lock screen. It is drawn on your phone from the session already stored there; it is not sent to us or to Apple, and the app does not ask for a push token to update it.

Blocking apps

To lock your screen during a session, the app uses Apple's Screen Time system (Family Controls). It asks Apple's system to shield your apps as a whole — it does not ask which apps you actually have, and it never receives a list of them. You approve this once, in a system dialog, and can take it back at any time from this app's own Settings screen — the "Turn off Screen Time" switch there ends any running session, unshields every app, and hands the permission back.

Who receives data, at a glance

  • Apple — takes the payment when you buy Uzak Pro. Always. Apple's privacy policy.
  • RevenueCat — checks your purchase so Pro unlocks and restores. Always. RevenueCat's privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase (Crashlytics and Remote Config) — crash reports and the app's settings. Always. Firebase privacy and Google's privacy policy.
  • Amplitude — usage statistics and an approximate location (country or city, from your IP address). Only if you say yes, and you can turn it off in Settings. Amplitude's privacy policy.

RevenueCat, Firebase Crashlytics and Amplitude all file what they receive under one random ID that the app makes the first time it opens: the app ID. It is not your name, your email, your Apple Account or Apple's advertising or device identifiers. It is stored in your phone's Keychain, only on this device: it stays the same if you delete and reinstall Uzak, it is not synced to your other devices, and a second phone gets its own. Amplitude receives it only after you allow usage statistics. Because the same ID is used in all three services, we can see, for example, that a crash happened to someone who had bought Pro. You can replace it at any time with "Reset ID" in the app's Settings; see "Turning statistics off, and asking for deletion" below.

Nothing in any of the sections below comes from Screen Time: the app never receives the list of your apps, and it never sends the name of an app, how long you use anything, or anything you did in another app.

Purchases (Apple and RevenueCat)

If you buy Uzak Pro, Apple's own purchase system handles the payment. We never see your card, your name, your email or your Apple Account. The purchase is then verified through RevenueCat, a purchase-management service, so the app can unlock Pro and restore it on your other devices. RevenueCat receives the purchase record Apple issues — which product, when it was bought, whether it is still active or in a free trial — along with basic technical details such as your iOS version, the app version and your App Store country. All of it is filed under the app ID described above. That ID is not tied to your name or your Apple Account; at RevenueCat it only keeps your purchases together. This is needed for Pro to work, so it happens whether or not you allow usage statistics.

Usage statistics (Amplitude) — only if you say yes

The first time you open Uzak it asks whether it may send usage statistics. If you say yes, the app sends events to Amplitude, an analytics service: when you open and close the app, which setup and purchase screens you reach, whether a purchase or restore started, went through, was cancelled or failed, how long your focus sessions run (in rounded steps, such as 15, 30 or 60 minutes), whether your city reached the free limit, and whether you have Pro. Each event carries the app ID, a second random ID that Amplitude makes on your phone, your language, phone model, iOS version and the app version. Neither is Apple's advertising or device identifier.

Amplitude also receives the IP address the statistics are sent from, and uses it to estimate an approximate location: your country, region or city. The app never asks for location permission and never sends your precise location.

We use these statistics to see where people get stuck during setup, whether focus sessions work as intended, and which offers people choose. If you say no, nothing is sent to Amplitude and it is never started. You can change your answer at any time with "Share usage statistics" in the app's Settings; when you turn it off, nothing more is sent.

Crash reports and settings (Google Firebase)

If the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics receives a crash report: the technical state of the app when it failed, details about the phone such as its model and iOS version, the app ID and a Firebase installation ID. Firebase also notes each time the app starts, so crash rates can be counted. When the app opens it asks Firebase Remote Config for settings we can change without a new release (today, how often the offer to upgrade may appear); that request carries the installation ID, your country code, language, time zone, iOS version and the app's identifier. This is how we find and fix crashes. It is sent whether or not you allow usage statistics, and it can't be switched off from inside the app. Uzak does not include Google Analytics for Firebase or any advertising product. Crashes in the parts of the app that run outside it (the shield and the session timer) are not reported to Firebase.

Separately, if you have turned on sharing with app developers in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements), Apple may pass us crash reports it collected. That is Apple's setting, not the app's.

What the outside services may do with it, and for how long

Amplitude, Google and RevenueCat receive this data only to run the features described above. We don't sell it, use it for advertising, or combine it with data from other companies to follow you across other apps or websites. The app ID links the records of these three services to each other only; it is never shared with other apps or with advertisers. Uzak does not track you in the sense Apple's App Tracking Transparency defines, and never asks for that permission. Each service processes the data for us under its own data processing terms, which commit it to security measures and hold it responsible for the companies it uses.

How long it is kept: Crashlytics crash reports are kept for 90 days. The Firebase installation ID used for settings requests is kept until we delete it, and Google then removes it within 180 days. RevenueCat does not publish a fixed retention period; it keeps purchase records for as long as they are needed to provide your purchases. For usage statistics, the plan we use shows us one year of Amplitude data.

Turning statistics off, and asking for deletion

To stop usage statistics, turn off "Share usage statistics" in the app's Settings. Crash reports and purchase checks can't be turned off in the app; they stop when you delete it.

To have data deleted, open Settings in Uzak and tap "Copy support IDs", then email what it copied to uzayaltiner@gmail.com. It copies two random IDs: the app ID, which finds your records at RevenueCat, Amplitude and Crashlytics, and your Firebase installation ID, which finds the settings requests Firebase received. Because none of these services knows your name, these IDs are the only way we can find your records. Turn off "Share usage statistics" before you send them, because Amplitude accepts new events even from a user whose records were deleted.

With those IDs we delete your user in Amplitude and your customer record in RevenueCat within 30 days of your email. Deleting the RevenueCat record does not take away what you bought: the purchase itself is Apple's, and "Restore Purchases" in the app gets Pro back from Apple at any time. For Google (Firebase) we request deletion within 30 days, and Google completes it within up to 180 days; crash reports also expire on their own after 90 days. Purchase records held by Apple are handled through your Apple Account. Deleting the app deletes your sessions and your city, but not what was already sent, and not the app ID: it stays in your phone's Keychain so a reinstall keeps the same ID. Use "Reset ID" to replace it.

You can also cut the link yourself: "Reset ID" in the app's Settings deletes the app ID from your phone and makes a new one, and from then on RevenueCat, Crashlytics and Amplitude receive only the new ID. What was already sent under the old ID stays with those services until it expires or is deleted, so to have it removed, tap "Copy support IDs" and email us before you tap "Reset ID". Resetting does not take away Pro: the purchase is Apple's, and "Restore Purchases" brings it back under the new ID.

Backups

The app does not mark its data to be skipped by device backups. So if you back up your phone to iCloud or a computer, that backup most likely includes this small amount of local data, the same as it would for most other apps' local storage. We could not confirm Apple's exact current policy on this from their own documentation while writing this page, so treat this as our honest best understanding rather than a guarantee.

Links that leave the app

A few places in the app hand a link to your browser or mail app: this policy, Apple's standard terms, and the address below. Opening one is a request made by that other app on your behalf, not by us, and we see nothing of it.
